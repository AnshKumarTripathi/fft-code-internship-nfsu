RESEARCH PAPER READING LIST
============================
Curated for: Fourier/signal processing foundations, Fourier in ML, transformers/attention,
information theory, and log-based anomaly detection (directly relevant to the log
anomaly detection SDD).

Note: "Claude Shannon" (Claude Elwood Shannon) is the founder of information theory —
same first name as Anthropic's Claude, no other relation.


1. FOURIER TRANSFORM & SIGNAL PROCESSING — FOUNDATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------

Cooley, J.W. & Tukey, J.W. (1965)
"An Algorithm for the Machine Calculation of Complex Fourier Series"
The original Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) paper — the algorithm that made Fourier
analysis computationally practical.
https://web.stanford.edu/class/cme324/classics/cooley-tukey.pdf

Gabor, D. (1946)
"Theory of Communication"
Introduces the windowed/time-localized Fourier transform (the "Gabor transform"),
the conceptual ancestor of the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) used in the
spectral log-anomaly detector.
https://digital-library.theiet.org/doi/10.1049/ji-3-2.1946.0074

Shannon, C.E. (1949)
"Communication in the Presence of Noise"
Introduces the sampling theorem (often called Nyquist-Shannon) — the formal basis
for why signals must be evenly sampled before an FFT/STFT is meaningful, directly
relevant to the log-bucketing step of the pipeline.
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1697831

Ahmed, N., Natarajan, T., & Rao, K.R. (1974)
"Discrete Cosine Transform"
Introduces the DCT, the frequency-domain transform underlying JPEG image compression
and closely related to the FFT.
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1672377


2. INFORMATION THEORY
--------------------------------------------------------

Shannon, C.E. (1948)
"A Mathematical Theory of Communication"
The founding paper of information theory: entropy, channel capacity, the bit as a
unit of information. Arguably the most influential single paper in computer science.
https://archive.org/details/bstj27-3-379
(readable PDF mirror: https://www.essrl.wustl.edu/~jao/itrg/shannon.pdf)


3. FOURIER TRANSFORM IN MACHINE LEARNING
--------------------------------------------------------

Vaswani, A. et al. (2017)
"Attention Is All You Need"
Introduces the Transformer architecture, and with it, sinusoidal positional
encoding — sine/cosine waves at multiple frequencies used to inject sequence order
into a model with no inherent notion of position.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762

Su, J. et al. (2021)
"RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)"
The positional encoding scheme used in most modern LLMs (LLaMA and others) — encodes
relative position as a rotation angle at multiple frequencies, a more direct use of
Fourier-style machinery than the original sinusoidal encoding.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864

Tancik, M. et al. (2020)
"Fourier Features Let Networks Learn High Frequency Functions in Low Dimensional Domains"
Shows that mapping inputs through sine/cosine features before feeding them to a
neural network dramatically helps it learn fine spatial detail — foundational to
NeRF-style 3D scene reconstruction.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10739

Li, Z. et al. (2020)
"Fourier Neural Operator for Parametric Partial Differential Equations"
Uses the Fourier transform inside a neural network layer to learn solution operators
for PDEs, orders of magnitude faster than traditional solvers.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895

Lee-Thorp, J. et al. (2021)
"FNet: Mixing Tokens with Fourier Transforms"
Replaces the attention mechanism in a Transformer with a literal (parameter-free)
Fourier transform, and shows it still works surprisingly well — a nice illustration
of how much of what attention does is really just mixing information across positions.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03824


4. FOUNDATIONAL DEEP LEARNING (BROADER CONTEXT)
--------------------------------------------------------

He, K. et al. (2015)
"Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition" (ResNet)
Introduces residual/skip connections, which made training very deep networks
practical and underlie most modern architectures, including Transformers.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385

Goodfellow, I. et al. (2014)
"Generative Adversarial Networks"
Introduces GANs — two networks trained adversarially against each other.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661

Devlin, J. et al. (2018)
"BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding"
Popularized the pretrain-then-finetune paradigm for NLP using Transformers.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805

Brown, T. et al. (2020)
"Language Models are Few-Shot Learners" (GPT-3)
Showed that scaling a Transformer language model far enough produces strong
few-shot/in-context learning without task-specific fine-tuning.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165

Ho, J., Jain, A., & Abbeel, P. (2020)
"Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models"
The paper underlying modern image-generation diffusion models (Stable Diffusion,
DALL-E 2 and successors build on this).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239


5. LOG PARSING & LOG-BASED ANOMALY DETECTION (directly relevant to the project)
--------------------------------------------------------

He, P., Zhu, J., Zheng, Z., & Lyu, M.R. (2017)
"Drain: An Online Log Parsing Approach with Fixed Depth Tree"
The template-mining algorithm the pipeline's parsing stage is built around.
https://jiemingzhu.github.io/pub/pjhe_icws2017.pdf

Xu, W., Huang, L., Fox, A., Patterson, D., & Jordan, M.I. (2009)
"Detecting Large-Scale System Problems by Mining Console Logs"
Early, influential paper on turning parsed console/system logs into features for
automated anomaly detection — a direct ancestor of the correlation/scoring stage
in the SDD.
https://doi.org/10.1145/1629575.1629587

Du, M., Li, F., Zheng, G., & Srikumar, V. (2017)
"DeepLog: Anomaly Detection and Diagnosis from System Logs through Deep Learning"
Treats a log as a sequence (like a sentence) and uses an LSTM to learn normal
sequences, flagging deviations — the conceptual basis for the ML detector stage.
https://doi.org/10.1145/3133956.3134015

Zhu, J. et al. (2019)
"Tools and Benchmarks for Automated Log Parsing"
The paper introducing LogHub / benchmarking a wide set of log parsers on real
datasets — the standard reference dataset collection for this kind of work.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03509

Meng, W. et al. (2019)
"LogAnomaly: Unsupervised Detection of Sequential and Quantitative Anomalies in
Unstructured Logs"
Combines semantic (content) and quantitative (count-based) signals for log anomaly
detection — a good illustration of the "combine multiple detector types" philosophy
used in the SDD's correlation stage.
https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0658.pdf
